The Complete Guide to Renting an
Exotic Car in Los Angeles

By Ali Hojat, Founder — First Class Exotics  ·  9 min read  ·  Updated May 2026

Los Angeles is one of the greatest cities in the world to drive an exotic car. The car culture runs deeper here than anywhere else in America — it is embedded in the city’s identity, its geography, and its very atmosphere. The roads range from legendary canyon passes to iconic coastal boulevards, and nowhere else on earth will you pull up to a valet in a Lamborghini and feel more completely, utterly at home. Here is everything you need to know to make your Los Angeles exotic rental perfect.

Best Neighborhoods to Experience in an Exotic

Beverly Hills is the undisputed heart of LA luxury car culture. Rodeo Drive, Wilshire Boulevard, and the surrounding tree-lined residential streets are where the world’s most prestigious vehicles come to be seen. Valets at the Peninsula, the Beverly Wilshire, and Montage Beverly Hills see everything — and a Lamborghini or Rolls-Royce still turns heads. This is where exotic car ownership and rental culture intersect most visibly, and where driving one feels most natural and most celebrated.

West Hollywood and the Sunset Strip are high-energy, high-visibility, and perfect for a vibrant evening arrival. The energy here matches a Lamborghini Huracán perfectly — loud, unapologetic, and electric. Thursday through Saturday nights, the Strip is alive in a way that makes every stoplight feel like a stage.

Malibu is the crown jewel for pure natural beauty combined with Pacific Coast Highway driving. The stretch from Santa Monica through Pacific Palisades to Point Dume is one of the most stunning coastal drives on the planet. Nobu Malibu, Soho House Malibu, and Geoffrey’s offer world-class dining with Pacific views and valet stands that see some of the most spectacular cars in Southern California.

Calabasas and Hidden Hills are the epicenter of luxury SUV culture in LA. The Lamborghini Urus is practically a neighborhood vehicle here, and arriving in one feels genuinely at home. The Calabasas Commons and the surrounding streets offer relaxed, high-net-worth vibes without the intensity of Beverly Hills.

Santa Monica and Venice offer a different energy — coastal, creative, and effortlessly cool. Ocean Avenue at sunset with a Ferrari is a photograph that will outlive your phone. The Third Street Promenade and Abbot Kinney Boulevard offer great dining and browsing with the car nearby.

Best Drives in the LA Area

Mulholland Drive is the legendary canyon road running along the crest of the Santa Monica Mountains above the city. Winding, dramatic, and offering jaw-dropping views of both the San Fernando Valley and the LA Basin in a single glance, Mulholland is a bucket-list drive for any serious car enthusiast. Best driven on a clear weekday morning before traffic accumulates and before afternoon haze builds over the valley.

Malibu Canyon Road connects PCH to the 101 freeway through the Santa Monica Mountains and is technically the more interesting drive of the two. Sweeping curves, canyon walls, and minimal traffic make this one of the best driving roads in all of Southern California. In a Ferrari F8 or McLaren, it is genuinely extraordinary.

Pacific Coast Highway from Santa Monica to Point Dume is the most beautiful stretch of PCH in the Los Angeles area. The approximately 25-mile drive passes through Pacific Palisades, Malibu, and out toward Zuma Beach with the Pacific ocean right beside you and the Santa Monica Mountains rising dramatically to your left.

Angeles Crest Highway is for the adventurous driver. Climbing into the San Gabriel Mountains above La Cañada Flintridge and Pasadena, this road offers serious technical driving with breathtaking alpine scenery. In the right car on a clear day, it is among the most dramatic driving experiences accessible from any major city in America.

Timing Is Everything — How to Beat LA Traffic

Los Angeles traffic is legendary for good reason, and there is nothing more frustrating than sitting in gridlock in a Lamborghini. Strategic timing makes the difference between an exhilarating day and an aggravating one.

  • Canyon drives (Mulholland, Malibu Canyon): Weekday mornings 7–10am are ideal
  • Beverly Hills cruising: Saturday or Sunday 10am–1pm before restaurant lunch crowds
  • PCH to Malibu: Early Sunday morning is genuinely magical — minimal traffic, golden hour light
  • Sunset Strip: Thursday–Saturday evenings after 7pm when the energy peaks
  • Absolutely avoid: Friday afternoon 2–7pm on any freeway. It cannot be overstated.
  • Pro tip: Use surface streets through Beverly Hills, Bel Air, and Brentwood rather than freeways. Slower but infinitely more scenic and satisfying.

What to Bring and What to Know

  • Valid U.S. driver’s license (must be 25 or older)
  • Personal auto insurance covering rental vehicles (call your agent first to verify)
  • A major credit card for the security deposit hold
  • Comfortable, flat-soled driving shoes
  • A phone mount for navigation — never hold your phone while driving an exotic
  • Knowledge of the fuel type required (most exotics require 91+ octane premium)
